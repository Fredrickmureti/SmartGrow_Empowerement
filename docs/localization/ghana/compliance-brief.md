# Ghana Payroll & Statutory Compliance Brief
### ERP Localization Reference | Effective Date: 2025 (updated August 2025)
### Prepared by: Senior Payroll Compliance Analyst

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Ghana operates a mandatory three-tier pension architecture (SSNIT Tier 1 + mandatory occupational Tier 2 + voluntary Tier 3) layered on top of a graduated PAYE system with rates from 0% to 35%. The 2025 SSNIT maximum insurable earnings ceiling rose from GHS 52,000 to **GHS 61,000 per month** (effective January 2025), directly increasing maximum Tier 1 contributions. PAYE bands remain as amended by Act 1094 (2023) with a top marginal rate of 35% on annual income exceeding GHS 605,000; non-residents are taxed at a flat 25%. Special concessional rates apply to qualifying junior employee overtime (5%/10%) and to bonus payments up to 15% of annual basic salary (5% final tax). Monthly PAYE and SSNIT Tier 1 remittance deadlines are the 15th and 14th of the following month respectively; Tier 2 is also due by the 14th. Genuine redundancy pay is fully exempt from income tax under Act 896.

## 4. PAYE — Personal Income Tax

**Governing law:** Income Tax Act, 2015 (Act 896), as amended by Acts 924, 941, 956, 967, 973, 1007, 1017, 1029, 1071, 1094 (2023), 1130, and 1155.  
**Source:** PwC Ghana Tax Summaries (current to 2025): https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/ghana/individual/taxes-on-personal-income; KPMG Ghana Tax DataCard August 2025: https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/gh/pdf/KPMG-2025-Tax-Datacard-Ghana.pdf

### 4.1 Resident Employee — Annual Graduated Bands (GHS)

| Chargeable Income Band (Annual, GHS) | Rate | Cumulative Tax (GHS) |
|---|---|---|
| First 5,880 | 0% | 0.00 |
| Next 1,320 (5,881–7,200) | 5% | 66.00 |
| Next 1,560 (7,201–8,760) | 10% | 222.00 |
| Next 38,000 (8,761–46,760) | 17.5% | 6,872.00 |
| Next 192,000 (46,761–238,760) | 25% | 54,872.00 |
| Next 366,240 (238,761–605,000) | 30% | 164,744.00 |
| Exceeding 605,000 | 35% | — |

> **Note:** The top band threshold is stated as "Exceeding 605,000" in the KPMG Aug 2025 DataCard; PwC Tax Summaries states "Exceeding 600,000." The KPMG figure (GHS 605,000) is more precise and consistent with the cumulative total of the band figures above. **[Verify exact threshold against GRA First Schedule to Act 896 as most recently amended].**

|---|---|

*Source: KPMG Ghana Tax DataCard August 2025*

### 4.3 Non-Resident Flat Rate
Non-resident individuals deriving employment income sourced in Ghana are taxed at a **flat 25%** on gross income.  
*Source: Act 896 First Schedule para. 2; PwC Tax Summaries (Ghana)*

### 4.4 Bonus Tax Treatment
**Governing regulation:** Income Tax Regulations, 2016 (L.I. 2244), Regulation 5(2)  
*Source: GNPC Ghana copy of L.I. 2244: https://www.gnpcghana.com/download/pl/INCOME_TAX_REGULATIONS_2016_.pdf*

| Condition | Tax Rate | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| Bonus ≤ 15% of annual basic salary | **5%** withheld at source | **Final tax** — not included in chargeable income; no reliefs apply |
| Bonus in excess of 15% of annual basic salary | Excess added to employment income → taxed at normal graduated rates per First Schedule | — |

**Calculation basis:** The 15% threshold is measured against the employee's annual basic salary for the year of assessment to which the bonus relates. Multiple bonus payments during the year are aggregated.

### 4.5 Overtime Concessional Tax Treatment
**Governing regulation:** L.I. 2244, Regulation 5(1) and 5(4)–5(5)

**Qualifying Junior Employee (QJE):** A junior staff member whose qualifying annual employment income does **not exceed GHS 18,000** for the year of assessment.

| Overtime Amount | QJE Rate | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 50% of monthly basic salary | **5%** | Final tax |
| Portion exceeding 50% of monthly basic salary | **10%** on the excess | Final tax |

**Non-QJE employees:** Overtime is included in total employment income and taxed at normal graduated PAYE rates. There is no concessional treatment.

### 4.6 Personal Reliefs
**Governing law:** Income Tax Act, 2015 (Act 896), Section 51; L.I. 2244, Regulation 10  
**Source:** PwC Deductions page: https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/ghana/individual/deductions; KPMG Aug 2025 DataCard

Employees must obtain a **Tax Reliefs Card** from the GRA Commissioner-General and furnish it to their employer to have reliefs applied in payroll.

| Relief Category | Annual Amount (GHS) | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Marriage / Responsibility Relief | 1,200 | Individual with a dependent spouse **or** at least two dependent children |
| Child Education Relief | 600 per dependent child/ward | Maximum **3** dependants; child must attend a recognised registered educational institution in Ghana |
| Aged Dependant Relief | 1,000 per dependant | Dependent relative (other than child or spouse) aged **60+**; maximum **2** dependants |
| Old Age (Self) Relief | 1,500 | Individual aged **60 or more** |
| Disability Relief | **25%** of assessable income from employment and business | Individual with a disability; replaces/supplements standard reliefs |
| Professional/Technical/Vocational Training | Up to 2,000 | Cost of training to update professional, technical or vocational skills |

**Non-residents are not entitled to any personal reliefs** (L.I. 2244, Regulation 2).

### 4.7 Pension Contribution Relief
Employee contributions to Tier 1 (5.5% of basic salary) and Tier 2 (employee's share if any — typically nil for mandatory Tier 2) and qualifying Tier 3 contributions are **deductible from chargeable income** under Section 112 of Act 766 and Act 896. Evidence of contractual pension arrangement must be submitted to the employer (L.I. 2244, Reg. 4(4)(b)).

### 4.8 Benefits in Kind
*Source: KPMG Ghana Tax DataCard August 2025*

| Benefit | Value Added to Chargeable Income |
|---|---|
| Driver + vehicle + fuel | 12.5% of total cash emoluments; max GHS 1,500/month |
| Vehicle + fuel | 10% of total cash emoluments; max GHS 1,250/month |
| Vehicle only | 5% of total cash emoluments; max GHS 625/month |
| Fuel only | 5% of total cash emoluments; max GHS 625/month |
| Accommodation with furnishing | 10% of total cash emoluments |
| Accommodation only | 7.5% of total cash emoluments |
| Shared accommodation | 5% of total cash emoluments; max GHS 625/month |

### 4.9 Mortgage Interest Relief
Mortgage interest on **one** residential property is deductible from chargeable income (limited to lifetime of the individual). *Source: Act 896 Sixth Schedule para. 4; KPMG Aug 2025 DataCard.*

## 5. SSNIT Tier 1 — Mandatory Basic Social Security

**Governing law:** National Pensions Act, 2008 (Act 766) as amended by Act 883 (2014)  
**Authority:** SSNIT — https://www.ssnit.org.gh  
**Source:** SSNIT Public Notice 28 January 2025; EY Tax Alert 27 February 2025: https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-0571; CRS News Flash 24 February 2025: https://www.crs.co.za/news-flash-24-february-2025-ghana-social-security-contributions-ceiling/

| Parameter | Value (Effective January 2025) | Prior Value (2024) |
|---|---|---|
| **Employee contribution rate** | **5.5%** of basic salary | 5.5% |
| **Employer contribution rate** | **13%** of basic salary | 13% |
| **Total contribution** | **18.5%** of basic salary | 18.5% |
| **Monthly insurable earnings ceiling** | **GHS 61,000** | GHS 52,000 |
| **Maximum monthly SSNIT contribution** (combined 18.5%) | **GHS 11,285** (18.5% × 61,000) | ~GHS 9,620 |
| **Maximum Tier 1 employee contribution (5.5%)** | **GHS 3,355/month** | ~GHS 2,860 |
| **Maximum total SSNIT remittance (13% to SSNIT)** | **GHS 7,930/month** | ~GHS 6,760 |
| **Maximum Tier 1 annual contribution** | **GHS 8,235** (as stated by SSNIT/EY) | — |
| **Minimum monthly contribution** | **GHS 66.16** | — |

> **Note:** SSNIT receives 13.5% of the 18.5% total (i.e., 5.5% EE + 8% of the 13% ER) for Tier 1 defined-benefit, and the remaining 5% of the 18.5% (from the employer's 13%) flows to Tier 2. The EY/SSNIT notice describes the "maximum SSNIT contribution" as GHS 8,235 — this appears to reference the Tier 1 portion only. **[Verify exact allocation split: Tier 1 vs. Tier 2 within the 13% ER contribution].**

**Contribution basis:** Basic salary only (not allowances, bonuses, or benefits in kind).

## 6. Tier 2 — Mandatory Fully Funded Occupational Pension

**Governing law:** National Pensions Act, 2008 (Act 766), Sections 3(3), 96, and 112  
**Authority:** NPRA — https://npra.gov.gh  
**NPRA Guidelines:** https://npra.gov.gh/assets/documents/Guidelines-for-the-payment-of-contributions.pdf

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| **Contribution rate** | **5%** of basic salary (sourced from within the employer's 13% total contribution) |
| **Payer** | Employer (funded from within the ER 13%; not an additional cost to the employer beyond 13%) |
| **Recipient** | NPRA-licensed Trustee of the employee's occupational pension scheme |
| **Basis** | Basic salary; same ceiling as Tier 1 (GHS 61,000/month for 2025) |
| **Remittance deadline** | **14th day of the following month** |
| **Documentation** | Monthly Contribution Report + Monthly Remittance Statement submitted to Trustee by last working day of the month; Custodian Notification Form (CNF) with payment |
| **Payment channel** | Banker's draft or cheque to the named NPRA-licensed Pension Fund Custodian |
| **Penalty for late payment** | Fine of 2,000 penalty units and/or 2 years imprisonment (Act 766, s.3(10)) |

**Employer obligation:** Deduct at source, hold in trust, and remit to the scheme trustee within 14 days. Employee enrolment in a scheme is mandatory upon commencement of employment.

## 7. Tier 3 — Voluntary Provident Fund and Personal Pension

**Governing law:** National Pensions Act, 2008 (Act 766); Income Tax Act, 2015 (Act 896), Section 112  
**Authority:** NPRA — https://npra.gov.gh

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| **Nature** | Voluntary, fully funded, privately managed |
| **Who contributes** | Employee and/or employer (any combination) |
| **Tax relief limit (combined Tier 2 + Tier 3)** | Up to **16.5%** of basic salary is tax-relieved [**verify:** some sources refer to the full 18.5% less Tier 1 EE 5.5% = 13%; the 16.5% figure reflects a specific legislative cap — **verify against current Act 896 s.112 and any amendments**] |
| **Additional employer Tier 3 contributions** | Deductible as a business expense for the employer |
| **Employee Tier 3 contributions** | Deductible from chargeable income up to the permitted limit |
| **Licensed vehicles** | NPRA-licensed Trustees run provident funds, personal pension schemes, and group life schemes |
| **Withdrawal tax** | Early withdrawals before qualifying age may attract tax; lump-sum at retirement may be exempt or partially exempt [**verify current rules**] |

## 8. Student Loan Trust Fund (SLTF) — Employer-Mediated Deductions

**Governing law:** Students Loan Trust Fund Act, 2011 (Act 820), Section 24  
**Authority:** SLTF — https://www.sltf.gov.gh

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Employer obligation** | Employers are legally required to deduct student loan repayments from employees' salaries where SLTF has notified the employer that the employee is a loan borrower/defaulter |
| **Trigger** | SLTF serves written notice on the employer identifying the employee and the repayment schedule |
| **Deduction amount** | As specified in the SLTF repayment schedule served on the employer |
| **Remittance** | Deducted amounts must be paid to SLTF; frequency and deadline specified in SLTF notice |
| **Penalty for employer non-compliance** | Employer of a defaulter who fails to make deductions is liable to prosecution under Act 820 s.24; SLTF has commenced prosecutions since October 2023 |
| **Employer "Good Standing" status** | SLTF publishes a list of compliant employers; non-compliant employers face reputational and legal risk |
| **Documentation** | SLTF notification letter; payroll deduction schedule per employee |

> **Payroll system note:** Build a separate deduction field for SLTF. It is not a tax (not routed through GRA); it is a debt recovery mechanism deducted alongside salary and remitted directly to SLTF.

## 9. End-of-Service / Redundancy — Tax Treatment

**Governing law:** Income Tax Act, 2015 (Act 896); Labour Act, 2003 (Act 651), Section 65  
**Source:** Deloitte Ghana analysis: https://www.deloitte.com/gh/en/services/tax/perspectives/when-is-compensation-received-upon-termination-of-employment-taxable.html

| Payment Type | Tax Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Genuine redundancy pay** (as defined under Labour Act, s.65 — closure, amalgamation, or reorganisation causing job loss) | **Exempt from income tax** — explicitly excluded from chargeable income under Act 896 |
| **Lump sum on genuine redundancy** | Exempt |
| **Resignation by own volition** (no management-compelled layoff) | Taxable as employment income at graduated PAYE rates |
| **Accrued leave pay** included in a package | **Taxable** — represents earned entitlement, not a redundancy payment |
| **Other end-of-service benefits** under contract | **Taxable** unless specifically exempted |
| **Unfair / non-qualifying termination payments** | Taxable even if labelled "redundancy" |

**Key principle:** The exemption flows from the characterisation of the payment, not its label. Employers must ensure that genuine redundancy proceedings comply with Labour Act Section 65 and that the compensation package separates exempt redundancy pay from taxable elements (accrued leave, PILON, etc.).

**PAYE mechanics on redundancy pay:** Where exempt, no withholding is required on that element. Any taxable elements (accrued leave, etc.) must be taxed at normal graduated rates in the final payroll run.

## 13. Payroll Frequency Norms

| Frequency | Prevalence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Monthly** | Dominant — formal sector standard | Most salaried employees; all statutory contributions computed monthly |
| **Bi-weekly / Fortnightly** | Uncommon | Mostly informal/casual labour; casual workers taxed at 5% final WHT |
| **Weekly** | Rare — mainly casual/construction | Casual worker provisions apply (L.I. 2244, Reg. 6) |
| **Daily** | Casual workers | 5% final tax on gross daily payment |

*There is no legislative mandated payroll frequency for formal employment; Labour Act, 2003 (Act 651) governs pay period agreements.*

## 15. Terminology Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Basic Salary** | The fixed contractual salary excluding all allowances, overtime, bonuses, and benefits; is the base for SSNIT, Tier 2 contributions, and overtime/bonus percentage calculations |
| **Cash Allowances** | Variable or fixed cash payments made to employees (e.g., transport, housing allowance, utility) in addition to basic salary; generally taxable as employment income unless specifically exempt |
| **Total Cash Emoluments** | Basic salary + all cash allowances + any other taxable cash; used as the base for Benefits-in-Kind assessment (motor vehicle, accommodation) |
| **Benefits in Kind (BIK)** | Non-cash advantages provided to an employee (vehicle, accommodation, loan concession); valued by statutory formula and added to chargeable income |
| **Chargeable Income** | Total assessable income from employment less deductible pension contributions and approved personal reliefs; the amount to which PAYE rates are applied |
| **Qualifying Employment Income** | Total of all amounts required to be included in computing an employee's income from employment for a year of assessment; used for overtime QJE threshold test |
| **Qualifying Junior Employee (QJE)** | A junior staff member whose qualifying annual employment income ≤ GHS 18,000; eligible for concessional overtime tax rates |
| **Qualifying Bonus** | Bonus up to 15% of annual basic salary; taxed at 5% final rate; excess added to employment income |
| **Insurable Earnings** | The basic salary on which SSNIT contributions are calculated; capped at GHS 61,000/month (2025) |
| **Tax Reliefs Card** | Document issued by GRA Commissioner-General to an employee certifying their personal reliefs; must be submitted to employer for reliefs to reduce PAYE withholding |
| **Redundancy Pay** | Lump-sum compensation to a worker whose employment is terminated due to closure, amalgamation, or reorganisation; exempt from income tax under Act 896 |
| **CNF (Custodian Notification Form)** | NPRA-prescribed form required to accompany every Tier 2 contribution payment to the pension fund custodian |
| **Year of Assessment** | Calendar year (1 January – 31 December) for individuals in Ghana |
